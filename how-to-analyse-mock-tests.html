  • Read accuracy and pace together.
  • Leave the review with a specific next task.

Sort each question into a useful category

Include correct guesses and skipped questions. They can explain more about your preparation than the total mark.

  • Concept gap: you could not explain the underlying idea. Revisit it before attempting more similar questions.
  • Method gap: you knew the concept but could not choose or finish the steps. Compare your approach with the worked solution.
  • Execution slip: the method was sound, but a sign, unit, calculation, or reading error changed the result.
  • Time decision: you spent too long, rushed, or skipped. Check how the choice affected the rest of the paper.

Read accuracy and timing together

Use the same definition each time you compare attempts. A percentage without a question count can be misleading.

  • Attempt accuracy: correct attempted questions ÷ attempted questions × 100. This is different from your percentage of total marks.
  • For example, 12 correct out of 20 attempted questions gives 60% attempt accuracy.
  • A result based on two questions tells you less than a repeated pattern across several attempts.
  • Compare papers with similar scope and difficulty; raw scores from very different tests are not a like-for-like measure.

Make a review note you can act on

Keep one short entry per recurring issue. Copy this structure into your notebook.

  • Question or chapter: what were you working on?
  • What happened: concept, method, execution, or time?
  • The missing step: explain it in one sentence.
  • Next task: name the topic and the practice you will do.
  • Recheck: return to a similar question later and solve it independently.

Use your VidyaRT report to find the evidence

Your report brings together subject and chapter performance, question review, and timing. Use it to choose a smaller, clearer next task.

  • Open a chapter result and inspect the questions behind it.
  • Compare your answer with the worked solution.
  • Check whether a slow question exposed a knowledge gap or an inefficient method.
  • Choose focused chapter practice, then review the next attempt.

Questions, answered.

What should I review after a mock test?

Review wrong answers, skipped questions, correct guesses, and questions that took longer than expected. Look for repeated concept, method, execution, or timing issues.

How do I calculate accuracy in a mock test?

For attempt accuracy, divide correct attempted questions by all attempted questions and multiply by 100. For example, 12 correct out of 20 attempted is 60%. Your exam score may use a different marking scheme.

What if my mock-test score drops?

Check the paper’s scope, difficulty, question count, and marking rules before comparing totals. Review the questions to see whether the change reflects a repeated gap or a different test.
